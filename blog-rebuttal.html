How to Structure a Winning Rebuttal

Sep 28, 2026 Marcus Thorne

A debate is rarely won during constructive speeches; it is won in the clash. A strong rebuttal doesn't just disagree with an opponent's point—it systematically dismantles the logic, evidence, and impact of their argument.

The 'Four-Step' Rebuttal Framework

1. They Say: Briefly summarize the opponent's argument so the judge knows exactly what you are attacking.
2. We Say: State your counter-claim clearly and concisely.
3. Because: Provide your evidence or logical warrant. Why is their claim fundamentally flawed? Is their evidence outdated, or does their logic rely on a false equivalency?
4. Therefore: Weigh the impact. Explain why winning this specific clash means you win the round.
